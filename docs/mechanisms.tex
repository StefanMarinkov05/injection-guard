A file reaches the agent only through the scanner: the hook denies raw reads of documents until \texttt{safe-read} has recorded the file's SHA-256 with a verdict, and keeps denying them if the verdict is HIGH or MED. The scanner prints the findings first and then only the text a human can see, between markers with a random nonce. Output of every other tool is checked after the fact.

\normalsize\section{Known limits}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item A visible, plausible decoy is indistinguishable from a real task - the scanner reports, a human or the agent must compare.
\item Phrase and directive lists miss wording nobody anticipated; structural, Unicode, encoding, render, OCR and ambiguity checks do not depend on wording.
\item Content produced at runtime (JavaScript, server per visitor) is flagged, not executed; browser snapshots are scanned by the output hook.
\item OCR is noisy on mathematics: single-symbol differences in formulas are not reported unless they repeat (systematic swap) or sit in a sentence.
\item Glyph-shape swaps inside an embedded font that keep a consistent text layer are only caught by OCR, and only on OCR'd pages (default: first 30).
\item QR codes, audio, video and archives inside archives are not decoded.
\item Hooks cannot rewrite built-in tool output; they block and redirect, or warn after the fact.
\end{itemize}

\item Never delete a test: a failing old test means a known attack works again.
\end{itemize}
